Prevention

10 Warning Signs of Heart Disease You Must Not Ignore

Chest discomfort, breathlessness, palpitations — these could be your heart asking for help. Learn the 10 early warning signs that every person above 35 should know.

1. Chest pressure or tightness — Not always severe pain. Many people feel a squeezing or heaviness.
2. Shortness of breath — Even with mild activity or while lying down.
3. Palpitations — Rapid, fluttering or irregular heartbeat.
4. Excessive sweating — Cold, clammy sweat without physical exertion.
5. Pain spreading to arm, jaw or back — Classic radiation pattern of cardiac pain.
6. Unexplained fatigue — Especially in women — feeling tired despite rest.
7. Dizziness or fainting — Reduced blood flow to brain.
8. Swollen ankles or feet — Sign of fluid retention / heart failure.
9. Persistent dry cough — Associated with heart failure.
10. Nausea or indigestion — Especially in women — often mistaken for stomach issues.

Dr. GS Gill advises: If you have 2 or more of these symptoms regularly, book an appointment for ECG and cardiac evaluation.

Tests

ECG vs 2D Echo vs TMT — Which Test Do You Need?

All three are heart tests — but which one does your doctor order and why? Simple explanation of the difference.

ECG (Electrocardiogram) — Records the electrical activity of your heart. Done in 5 minutes. Used for: Heart attack detection, rhythm disorders, palpitations. Cost: Very affordable.

2D Echocardiography — Ultrasound of the heart. Shows heart chambers, valves and pumping function. Used for: Heart failure, valve disease, heart size. Takes 20–30 minutes.

TMT (Treadmill Test / Stress Test) — ECG done while walking on treadmill. Detects blockages in heart arteries. Used for: Chest pain on exertion, suspected angina. Duration: 15–30 minutes.

Which one for you?
• Chest pain at rest → ECG first
• Breathlessness / heart failure → 2D Echo
• Chest pain on walking / exercise → TMT

All 3 are done at Gill Heart Clinic, Meerut.

Diet

Indian Diet for a Healthy Heart — What to Eat & Avoid

Simple swaps in your daily Indian diet that can dramatically reduce your heart disease risk — no fancy food needed.

✅ Heart-Healthy Indian Foods:
• Whole grains: Bajra, Jowar, Brown rice, Oats
• Dals and legumes: Masoor, Moong, Chana
• Vegetables: Palak, Methi, Lauki, Karela
• Fruits: Amla, Pomegranate, Guava, Papaya
• Healthy fats: Mustard oil, Walnut, Flaxseeds
• Spices: Haldi, Dalchini, Lahsun (garlic)

❌ Foods to Avoid / Reduce:
• Vanaspati / Dalda / Trans fats
• Maida (refined flour) products
• Packaged namkeen, chips, biscuits
• Excess salt (namak) — limit to 5g/day
• Sugary drinks — cold drinks, packaged juice
• Red meat excess

Dr. GS Gill's Tip: Ghee in moderation is okay. It is the trans fats (vanaspati) that are truly harmful for the heart.

Blood Pressure

High BP: The Silent Killer — Know Your Numbers

Blood pressure 140/90 or above is dangerous — yet most patients feel no symptoms. Know when to act.

Blood Pressure Categories:
• Normal: Below 120/80 mmHg
• Elevated: 120–129 / below 80
• Stage 1 High BP: 130–139 / 80–89
• Stage 2 High BP: 140/90 or above
• Hypertensive Crisis: 180/120+ — Seek care immediately

Why is high BP dangerous?
Sustained high pressure damages arteries silently over years, leading to:
• Heart attack
• Stroke
• Kidney damage
• Vision loss

Dr. GS Gill advises: Check your BP at home every week if you are above 40. A digital BP machine costs ₹1000–1500 and can save your life. Take medications regularly — never stop on your own.

Diabetes

Diabetes & Heart Disease: The Deadly Connection

Diabetic patients are 2–4 times more likely to develop heart disease. Here's what you must do differently.

How Diabetes Damages Your Heart:
High blood sugar damages blood vessel walls over time, causing:
• Atherosclerosis (hardening of arteries)
• Silent heart attacks (without chest pain)
• Heart failure
• Poor healing after cardiac events

Diabetic Heart Care Checklist:
✓ HbA1c test every 3 months — target below 7%
✓ Annual ECG and lipid profile
✓ BP monitoring — target below 130/80
✓ Cholesterol check — LDL below 100 mg/dL
✓ Quit smoking completely
✓ Walk 30 minutes daily

Dr. GS Gill advises: Every diabetic patient above 40 should get an annual non-invasive cardiac evaluation — ECG + 2D Echo + Lipid profile — even without symptoms.

Lifestyle

Best Exercises for a Healthy Heart — Safe for Indian Patients

Not all exercise is equal. Know which physical activities strengthen the heart without putting it at risk.

Best Cardiac-Safe Exercises:
• Brisk Walking — 30 min daily, 5 days/week. Most effective and safest.
• Cycling — Low impact, great for BP control.
• Swimming — Excellent for heart and joints.
• Yoga & Pranayam — Anulom-Vilom, Bhramari reduce stress and BP.
• Light weight training — 2–3 times/week under supervision.

Warning — Avoid these if unfit:
❌ Sudden intense exercise without warm-up
❌ Heavy weight lifting if BP uncontrolled
❌ Running in extreme heat

Dr. GS Gill advises: Get a TMT (Treadmill Test) done before starting any new exercise program if you are above 45, diabetic, hypertensive, or a smoker. Exercise is medicine — but the dose matters.
